HARDPAN

Privacy Policy

Last updated: 26 October 2026 · Rattle Golf LLC

HardPan is a golf app for private groups: GPS yardages, scoring, and the games your group plays. It is made by Rattle Golf LLC, and the App Store listing may say Rattle Golf. Rattle Golf Tournaments is a separate events product and is not covered by this policy. This policy explains what HardPan stores, why, and what it does not do.

The short version. HardPan keeps your group's golf: names, scores and the games you play. It makes you an anonymous account so the rounds you start stay yours. An email address is asked for only if you choose to sign in. Your GPS position is used on your phone to measure yardages and is not sent to us, with one exception you control (setting a green, below). If you choose to scan a paper scorecard, that one photo is sent to Google's Gemini service to read the scores, and HardPan does not keep it. No ads, no analytics, no tracking, and nothing is sold.

Microphone and voice scoring

  • Only when you ask. In the HardPan GPS app on iPhone you can say your score (for example "a 5, two putts") with the voice button, or through Siri. HardPan asks for microphone and Speech Recognition permission the first time, and the microphone is on only while you hold or tap the voice button.
  • On your phone where possible. HardPan uses Apple's speech recognizer and asks it to work on your iPhone whenever your iPhone supports that. On an iPhone that can't, Apple's speech recognition service turns the audio into words, under Apple's privacy policy. When you use Siri, Siri handles what you say the same way.
  • Nothing stored, nothing sent by HardPan. HardPan does not record or keep your voice, and it does not send audio or what you said to us or anyone else. The words become a score on the normal score sheet for you to check and save; only the score you save is stored, like any score you type. If you ask Siri for a score while your phone is locked, the words wait on your phone (for up to 2 hours) until you open your round.

Scorecard scan

  • The camera, only when you ask. HardPan uses the camera only when you tap to scan a paper scorecard. If you pick a photo instead, only the one picture you choose is read; HardPan does not browse your photo library.
  • What is sent. The photo goes to our server (Cloudflare) together with what the round already knows: the course name, tee, par and stroke index for each hole, and the round's player names (with any initials or nicknames). Handicaps, if the round has them, go to our server only, to check the result; they are not sent on. Our server sends the photo, the course details and the player names to Google's Gemini API, which reads the scores and sends them back. Your sign-in token is sent so the server knows the scan is yours; it is not passed to Google. Your location is never sent.
  • What is kept. HardPan does not store the photo. It is processed in memory and is not written to our database or our logs. Our server's log records only counts, timing, which model was used and whether the result checked out - no photo, no names. Nothing goes on the scorecard until you check the scores and tap to use them; then they are saved exactly as if you had typed them.
  • Google. HardPan uses the paid Gemini API. Under Google's Gemini API Additional Terms of Service for paid services, Google does not use your prompts or responses (including images) to improve its products, and logs them for a limited period of time only to detect and prevent violations of its Prohibited Use Policy, and for legal or regulatory disclosures where required by law (ai.google.dev/gemini-api/terms).

What is never collected

  • No password or phone number, and an email address only if you choose to sign in
  • Your GPS position is never sent to us, and background location is used only while a round's Lock Screen view is on (see Your location)
  • No contacts access, and no browsing of your photo library. The camera is used only when you choose to scan a scorecard (see Scorecard scan above). The microphone is used only for voice scoring, as described above, and never recorded.
  • No advertising identifier, and no third-party analytics or tracking SDK
  • No payment details of any kind — see Money, below

Your location

  • HardPan uses location only to show yardages: on the GPS screen while the app is open, and, if you turn on a round's Lock Screen view, while your phone is locked so the yardages on the Lock Screen stay current. That background use happens only while the Lock Screen view is on, stays on your phone, and stops when the round's Lock Screen view ends.
  • Your position stays on your phone. It turns into yardages on the screen and is not sent to us, stored in your account, or put in a link.
  • The one exception is "set the green here": that saves the spot you are standing on as the green, without your name or account.
  • "Near you" (nearby courses) is worked out on your phone.
  • You can switch location off at any time in your phone's settings. Scores, games and the scorecard work without it.

Money

HardPan records the games a group agreed to play and calculates the resulting total for each player. That is arithmetic, not a transaction.

The app does not accept wagers, hold funds, process payments, or pay winnings. HardPan never takes, holds or moves money between golfers; the "who owes whom" summary is information for your group. If money changes hands, it happens between the people involved, outside the app. HardPan never sees it and takes no share of it.

Where data is stored

Your account and round data are stored with Google Firebase (Firebase Authentication and Firebase Realtime Database), operated by Google Cloud. Google processes it for us as a service provider under its own security and privacy terms.

Services that help run HardPan

  • Google Firebase (Authentication, Realtime Database, Cloud Functions): your account, round data and course searches. Google processes it for us as a service provider.
  • GolfAPI (golfapi.io), through our server: the course name you search for and course ids. Never your position or name.
  • Esri (ArcGIS) and the U.S. Geological Survey (USGS): the satellite map on the GPS screen. Your phone asks them for map tiles of the course, and they see that request and your internet address. USGS also gives heights for points on the course (for "plays like"). If a course has no map data, the map can be centered where you stand, so Esri and USGS see tile requests for that area.
  • U.S. National Weather Service (api.weather.gov): the wind. Your phone sends the course's location, not yours, and the service sees your internet address.
  • Apple, on iPhone only: Sign in with Apple if you use it, and push notifications if you allow them (your device's notification token and your notification choices are stored so a round can notify you). Speech recognition for voice scoring and Siri runs on your iPhone where it can, and otherwise through Apple's speech service, as described under Microphone and voice scoring.
  • GolfCourseAPI (golfcourseapi.com), for published scorecards: see Third-party directories.
  • Cloudflare: runs our scorecard-search proxy and the scorecard-scan service.
  • Google Gemini API: only if you scan a scorecard. It receives that photo, the course name, tee, par and stroke index, and the round's player names, and returns the scores it reads. See Scorecard scan above.

Third-party directories

Scorecard search reaches golfcourseapi.com only through our own Cloudflare Worker. That provider receives the course name or town you type, to find a published scorecard. We may keep that search text on our proxy for a short time so the same lookup is not repeated. Your position is never sent. Round scores and player names are not sent to golfcourseapi.

Sharing

Data is not sold, rented, or shared for anyone's advertising. It goes only to the services above, for the reasons given, and where the law requires it.

Kept on your device only

Dark mode, your last roster, recent rounds and courses, which golfer is you, downloaded course maps and any scores waiting for a signal are kept on your phone (or in your browser) and are not sent to us, except that waiting scores are sent to the round once you're back online.

Who can see a round

Rounds are shared by link or code with the group. Anyone holding a round link can view that round, and the people you give a scorekeeper link can score it. Anyone who can guess a code could view it too. There is no public list of rounds and no public profile, so the practical guidance is simple: treat a round link like a group chat, and don't put anything in a player name you wouldn't want a stranger to read.

How long we keep it

Rounds are kept so your group can look back at them, until the organizer wipes the round or asks us to. Your account is kept until you delete it.

Deleting your data

  • Your account: in the app, open Account and tap Delete account (it appears once you have signed in; if you never signed in, see delete-account). That deletes your sign-in (email and account id) and your saved groups straight away. The delete-account page lists exactly what is deleted and what stays. It also erases your scorecard-scan counts (your account ID and how many scans you have used).
  • A whole round: the organizer can use "End & Wipe Round," which deletes that round's data.
  • On your device: clearing your browser's site data, or deleting the app, removes everything stored locally.
  • Anything else, or if you can't open the app: email support@rattlegolf.com from the address you signed in with (or with the round code). We'll reply within 7 days and delete within 30 days.

Children

HardPan is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect information from them. If you think a child's information is in HardPan, email us and we will delete it.

Security

Everything is sent over encrypted connections (HTTPS). Database rules keep each account's records to its owner. A round is open to anyone who has its code, as described under Who can see a round.
