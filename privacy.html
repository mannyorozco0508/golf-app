HARDPAN

Privacy Policy

Last updated: 24 October 2026

HardPan is a golf app for private groups: a live scorecard, the side games your group plays, a running tally of who owes who, and HardPan GPS yardages. Rattle Golf LLC operates HardPan and also Rattle Golf Tournaments, the separate events product, which this policy does not cover. This policy explains what HardPan stores, why, who else is involved, and how to delete it. It is written to be read, not to be survived.

The short version. You can use HardPan without telling us who you are. If you sign in with Apple, Google or an email link, we keep your email address so your rounds follow you. We store the golf information your group types in, so everyone sees the same card. Your phone's location is used on your phone for yardages; it is saved only when you choose to set a green where you are standing. HardPan has no ads, no advertising identifier and no analytics or tracking SDK, does not sell data, and never holds or moves money. You can delete your account inside the app.

Accounts and sign-in

  • Without signing in: the first time you open HardPan, Firebase Authentication (Google) gives the app an anonymous account so the rounds you set up stay yours. It has no name or email on it.
  • Sign in with Apple, Google, or an email link: Firebase Authentication keeps your email address (with Sign in with Apple this may be Apple's private relay address), which sign-in method you used, and, if Apple or Google shares it, your name. We use this only to sign you in and keep your rounds, saved groups, free trial or pass with you across devices. To send an email sign-in link, Firebase sends one email to the address you type.

Location

  • Yardages, on your phone. In HardPan GPS, the GPS screen uses your phone's location while that screen is showing, and for at most a minute after you switch back, to show your distance to the green and targets. That calculation happens on your phone. Your position is not sent to us or anyone else for this.
  • Courses near you. When you pick a GPS course, your phone uses its location to sort the course list by distance. That stays on your phone.
  • Setting a green - the one time a position is saved. If you set or fix a green with the Set button while standing on it, your phone's position at that moment is saved as that green's location. If you set it by tapping the map, the point you tapped is saved instead. Either way it is saved to the round and to the shared course record so other golfers on that course see the same green. It is saved only when you choose to do this, and it is stored as a spot on the course, not as a history of where you have been. A saved green carries no name and no account id - only the spot, the time it was set and how it was set (by GPS or by a tap).
  • Wind. Live wind is looked up for the course, using a point from the course data rounded to about 100 meters - never your own position. A HardPan build without the GPS screen does not ask for your location at all.

Microphone and voice scoring

  • Only when you ask. In the HardPan GPS app on iPhone you can say your score (for example "a 5, two putts") with the voice button, or through Siri. HardPan asks for microphone and Speech Recognition permission the first time, and the microphone is on only while you hold or tap the voice button.
  • On your phone where possible. HardPan uses Apple's speech recognizer and asks it to work on your iPhone whenever your iPhone supports that. On an iPhone that can't, Apple's speech recognition service turns the audio into words, under Apple's privacy policy. When you use Siri, Siri handles what you say the same way.
  • Nothing stored, nothing sent by HardPan. HardPan does not record or keep your voice, and it does not send audio or what you said to us or anyone else. The words become a score on the normal score sheet for you to check and save; only the score you save is stored, like any score you type. If you ask Siri for a score while your phone is locked, the words wait on your phone (for up to 2 hours) until you open your round.

What is never collected

  • No phone number, and no password: sign-in is through Apple, Google or an email link
  • No contacts, photos or camera access. The microphone is used only for voice scoring, as described above, and never recorded
  • No advertising identifier, and no third-party analytics or tracking SDK
  • No crash-reporting service: errors stay in your phone's own log
  • No payment details of any kind - see Money and Purchases below

Who else is involved

These services process data for HardPan as service providers. None of them receives your scores, player names or position except as stated here.

  • Google Firebase (Authentication, Realtime Database, Cloud Functions, Cloud Messaging): stores accounts and round data, runs our course and wind lookups, and delivers notifications.
  • Cloudflare: hosts the website and runs our course-search proxy and the service that sends notifications.
  • Apple: Sign in with Apple, push notifications on iPhone, the App Store for any purchase, and Apple Weather (WeatherKit) for live wind when it is switched on. Our server sends Apple the course point only. Speech recognition for voice scoring and Siri runs on your iPhone where it can, and otherwise through Apple's speech service, as described under Microphone and voice scoring.
  • Google Sign-In, if you choose Continue with Google.
  • GolfAPI (golfapi.io): for HardPan GPS course search, the text you type goes to our Cloud Function, which asks GolfAPI and saves the answer so the same search is not repeated.
  • golfcourseapi.com: the scorecard search on the round setup screen sends the text you type through our Cloudflare Worker to golfcourseapi.com to find a published scorecard. We may keep that search text on our proxy for a short time. Round scores and player names are not sent to golfcourseapi.
  • National Weather Service (api.weather.gov): when Apple Weather is off or unavailable, your phone asks the NWS for the wind at the course point, so the NWS sees that request and your internet address.
  • Esri (ArcGIS) and the U.S. Geological Survey (USGS): the satellite picture on the GPS screen. Your phone asks them for the map squares of the course you are looking at, the same way any web map works, so they see that request and your internet address. USGS squares for a course may be saved on your phone so the picture works without signal. Hole and green outlines from OpenStreetMap ship inside the app.

Data is not sold, rented, or shared for advertising. We also share data where the law requires it.

Notifications

HardPan asks before it sends notifications, and only once you open a round. If you allow them, the notification token for your device is stored under your account as described above, and notifications about your rounds (you're in a round, the final results, a side-game challenge or offer, and hype) are sent through Firebase Cloud Messaging and Apple's push service. You can turn the optional ones off in Account, or turn all of them off in your phone's settings.

Money

HardPan records the games a group agreed to play and works out who owes who. That is arithmetic, not a transaction.

The app does not hold funds, move money, process payments, or pay winnings. If money changes hands, it happens between the people involved, outside the app, using whatever they normally use. HardPan never sees it and takes no share of it.

Purchases

HardPan GPS (the GPS Map subscription) is bought through the App Store: Apple handles the payment, and we, and the purchase service we use to check subscriptions (RevenueCat), receive only what was bought, whether it is active, and the account it belongs to - never your card or payment details.

Who can see a round

Rounds are shared by link or code with the group. Anyone holding a round link can view that round, which is the point - you send it to your group. Anyone who can guess a code could view it too, so the practical guidance is simple: treat a round link like a group chat, and don't put anything in a player name you wouldn't want a stranger to read.

Children

HardPan is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect information from them.

Deleting your data

  • Your account, inside the app: open Account and tap Delete account. This deletes the account itself, your organizer record (including any free trial or pass), your saved groups, the co-organizer invites you sent, your notification tokens and settings, and your course-lookup counts. If you signed in with Apple, HardPan also asks Apple to revoke the app's access to your Apple ID.
  • Rounds stay in their round codes: deleting an account does not delete rounds, because other golfers may still be scoring them. The organizer can use "End & Wipe Round," which deletes that round's data.
  • Device settings: clearing your browser's site data, or deleting the app, removes everything stored locally.
  • Anything else: email the address below - with the round code, if it is about a round - and it will be deleted. That includes removing your account ID from the course-lookup log.
